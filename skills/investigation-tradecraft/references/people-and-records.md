# People, records and due diligence

The records-and-people side of investigation, as private investigators and due-diligence analysts practise it: getting from a database hit to the original record, confirming that a located person is the right one, tracing who really owns a company, and the desk work that comes before interviews and surveillance.

## Aggregators, indexes and original records

People-search products, "national criminal" databases and broker reports are useful for finding leads. What they say still has to be checked:

- **Brokers merge people and keep stale addresses.** "National" criminal databases have patchy county coverage.
- **An index isn't the record.** A court docket line or a broker's owner field shows that a record probably exists somewhere. It doesn't show what the record says or whose it is.
- **The issuing office settles it.** The court file, the recorder's deed (not just the assessor's roll) or the company registry entry can confirm what the broker summarised. Noting which office and the access date keeps the fact checkable.
- **Coverage is part of the result.** A clean database search says nothing about counties or registries the database doesn't hold.

## Matching a record to a person

A name match alone is not a match. US regulators have acted against background screeners for matching on first and last name only, and name plus date of birth can still collide. These tie a record to a specific person:
- date of birth;
- middle name or initial;
- suffix;
- an overlapping address history;
- other identifiers on the record itself.

Jr/Sr in the same household, and parents and children sharing a name, are the classic errors.

## Locating someone

A locate is a claim about one person at one place at one time. Skip tracers tend to build it in layers:

1. **The subject's identifier set**: names and variants, date of birth, known phones and emails, vehicles, prior addresses.
2. **A dated address history**, with each entry marked by its status:
   - confirmed (seen in an original record or verified);
   - corroborated (several independent sources agree);
   - reported (a single broker or tip);
   - conflicted.
3. **Corroboration from more than one independent source** before saying someone "resides at" an address.

Some distinctions that matter:

- **Different kinds of address.** A mailing address, a residence, a workplace and a one-off sighting are different findings.
- **Relatives and associates cut both ways.** Shared addresses, co-signers and co-defendants help confirm identity, but a name-twin error drags these same people in.
- **A gap in the address history is an open question.** It isn't evidence of flight, custody or death.
- **Who is asking matters as much as the method.** Finding someone and handing over where they are are separate decisions. See `references/legal-and-ethics.md` before releasing a location.

## Ownership, nominees and sanctions

A company filing shows who was declared, not who controls the company.

- **Nominees are a known structure.** Professional nominee directors and shareholders lend their names. ICIJ describes the usual paperwork behind them: an instruction agreement, a power of attorney and an undated resignation letter.
- **Where to look for the real controller:**
  - corporate directors;
  - circular or layered holdings;
  - mass-registration addresses;
  - the agent or law firm behind the filings;
  - powers of attorney;
  - payment flows;
  - addresses or officers shared with other companies.
- **Registry data quality varies.** UK Companies House only began verifying the identity of directors and people with significant control in November 2025, so earlier filings are self-declared. Many registries still verify nothing.
- **Sanctions exposure depends on aggregated ownership through the chain**, not just on whether a name appears on a list. Under OFAC's 50% rule:
  - holdings of blocked persons are added together;
  - the test is ownership, not control;
  - OFAC publishes no list of the entities the rule captures.
- **A leak database entry is a lead, not wrongdoing.** Offshore Leaks and similar databases show that an entity or person appeared in the material. The name still needs the identifier discipline above.

## Adverse media and litigation

- **Weigh pattern and recurrence over single hits.** Repeated, independent reporting on a subject means more than one article, and the original reporting matters more than reprints of it.
- **Same-name hits are the main noise.** Adverse-media matching needs the same identifier discipline as records matching.
- **An allegation is not a finding.** Litigation often settles without any finding. Read the filings themselves, not just the docket summary.

## Scoping from the decision

Practitioners usually scope a case from the decision the client has to make, not from the list of services ordered. Writing down that decision, and the few questions the report must answer, before collecting:
- sets the depth by what is at stake;
- shows when to stop;
- surfaces deadlines and evidence that may disappear.

A check-in at a natural stopping point helps more than spending hours because they're available.

## Desk work before interviews

Agents rarely interview anyone, but they can do the work on either side of an interview. The PEACE model, used by UK police, runs:
1. planning and preparation;
2. engage and explain;
3. account (clarify and challenge);
4. closure;
5. evaluate.

It is non-accusatory and aims to gather information.

Useful desk contributions are:
- **Before:** a plan listing what is already known, what is disputed, and what the account should be tested against.
- **After:** a comparison of the account with the record, noting where they agree, where they conflict, and what is new.

## Desk work before surveillance

Whether to go into the field is the human's decision. A lot of what makes surveillance succeed is desk work done beforehand:
- confirming the residence, since map pins can be wrong;
- the subject's vehicles;
- who else lives there and could be mistaken for the subject;
- a recent photo;
- whether the subject knows about the investigation.
